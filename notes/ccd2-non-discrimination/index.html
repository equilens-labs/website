Equilens checklist

Non-discrimination under CCD2

A 2-page checklist for risk and compliance teams at EU consumer lenders.

Member States must apply the new Consumer Credit Directive, (EU) 2023/2225 or “CCD2”, from 20 November 2026 (Art. 48). Under Article 6, the conditions for being granted credit must not discriminate against consumers legally resident in the EU on any ground listed in Article 21 of the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights, including sex, ethnic origin, disability and age. Different conditions of access remain possible where they are “duly justified by objective criteria”.

The information used to assess creditworthiness must not include special categories of personal data, such as ethnic origin or health (Art. 18(3)). Age is different: most lenders already hold date of birth. This checklist covers where to start, one credit decision at a time.

1. Scope one decision

Pick one decision in one product: approve or decline at application, credit limit, or price tier. Write down the population, the period and what counts as a favourable outcome. A gap measured across a mixed population is hard to explain.

2. Write down why each criterion is there

List the conditions that decide who gets credit and on what terms: score cut-offs, policy rules, affordability buffers and pricing tiers. For each one, record the objective reason for it. Article 6 allows different conditions of access where they are duly justified by objective criteria, so that record is your starting point. You must also document your assessment procedures (Art. 18(4)).

3. Use the characteristics you already hold

Start with age: date of birth gives you age at application, so you can compare age groups now. Some lenders also record sex. Check that using these data for testing has a lawful basis and is covered by your privacy notice.

4. Plan for missing data

For grounds you do not hold, such as ethnic origin or disability, look at complaints, customer research and reviews of the application journey. Inferring ethnic origin, for example from names, is likely to be processing of special category data (GDPR Art. 9(1)). That needs a lawful basis and an Article 9 condition, and probably a data protection impact assessment. Keep any such data out of the creditworthiness assessment itself (Art. 18(3)), and record the method you used and its limits.

5. Agree the comparison and the trigger in advance

Name the reference group, the measure (for example, the approval-rate ratio or the percentage-point gap) and the size of gap that triggers a review. Set a minimum group size too: small groups produce unstable gaps. Agree all of this with risk and compliance before you look at results.

6. Treat a gap as a question, not a verdict

Compare like with like first, for example applicants in the same risk band. A remaining difference can come from applicant mix, policy rules, data quality or manual overrides. Then ask whether the condition driving it is “duly justified by objective criteria” (Art. 6). Investigate before concluding, and record what you checked.

7. Write down what happens next

Record the owner, the action and the date of the next run, and keep the result with your documented assessment procedures. Re-run after material changes to policy, models or data. Where the assessment uses automated processing, consumers can ask for a clear explanation of it and a review of the decision (Art. 18(8)).

How Equilens can help

FL-BSA (Fair-Lending Bias-Simulation Appliance) is a customer-hosted, simulation-only evidence appliance. From a snapshot of your historical credit decisions, it generates synthetic borrower cohorts (artificial records that follow the patterns in your data) and reports approval-rate gaps between groups in that simulation: by sex, and by ethnicity where you hold or can lawfully derive it. Each run produces a report and a tamper-evident evidence pack. It does not report approval-rate gaps by age group; the age steps above rely on your own analysis. It does not make or override live lending decisions, provide legal advice, certify regulatory compliance or claim regulator approval.
