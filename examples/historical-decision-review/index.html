What your team supplies

A prepared file containing recorded decisions and comparison groups, the population and period being reviewed, and a clear definition of the outcome being counted.

Preparation and source-selection rules stay with the review. The demonstrated flow uses one cohort column, 2–20 declared groups and at least 30 rows in every group. This floor does not establish statistical adequacy. Borrower data stays in your environment.

What you can review

Group counts and recorded outcome rates, a findings document, a PDF summary and a saved package that can be checked locally using the retained prepared file, exact analysis specification and matching software version.

Source extraction is checked separately. The result describes your supplied records; it does not explain causes or predict future decisions.

A worked public-data example

This technical example uses public 2024 US mortgage records for Ally Bank. We chose Home Mortgage Disclosure Act (HMDA) data because its public loan-level records include lending outcomes and applicant sex, allowing this example to be checked without customer data. The lender did not participate in or endorse the exercise. It demonstrates a reporting workflow, not a UK/EU consumer-credit benchmark or a fairness verdict.

Reported origination or approval, by recorded applicant sex

What was checked?

For this declared example, a separate calculation reproduced the source selection, counts, rates and displayed results across 75 checks. The packaged workflow also created and checked the report offline, and rejected a changed input.

This checks the declared example. It does not establish customer time savings, results for other data or production availability.
